# Decision: ties break against our own agents and no payment ever moves position

Recorded by `07-MATCHING.md` decisions 12 and 14, with section 10 owning the audit.

## The decision

Where a first-party listing ties a third-party listing on the published function, the third party wins.
No seller payment, boost or fee moves a position, in v1 or later. No term in the ranking reads the
first-party list and the parameter vector is published so an outsider can check that.

## The alternative rejected

Strict neutrality on ties, which is what the neutrality clause alone would give. Also rejected: a
disclosed paid boost, which P2B Article 5(3) permits with a description of the possibilities plus their
effects. Also rejected: a priced exclusivity override where a named agent keeps the job unless somebody
beats it by a margin, which is a real mechanism in the field.

## Why

A coin flip between our listing and a stranger's is defensible plus unprovable to somebody who distrusts
us. The asymmetry costs us at most the ties and it cannot be gamed because nobody else can become
first-party.

A marketplace whose first revenue lever is placement has to defend every ranking claim it makes
afterwards. We would rather have nothing to disclose. The regulator's own rules make undisclosed paid
ranking unfair in all circumstances and a disclosed slot still poisons the one signal this product has,
which is a job somebody paid for.

The exclusivity override is refused for a sharper reason: it swaps out the agent the buyer named, which is
the one thing an override exists to prevent.

## What it binds

`02-THESIS.md`'s first-party disclosure clause 4, `07-MATCHING.md`'s anti-favouritism rules plus its
holdout, `06-QUALITY.md` where exploration is the only other way position moves, `08-MONEY.md`'s fee
which is charged per job rather than for placement, `14-GAPS.md` which refuses referral rewards, points
plus paid placement together.

## What would make us revisit it

Nothing on the paid placement. The tie-break asymmetry could retire once first-party rows stop existing
on a shelf, which is the direction the ratio is published to move.
