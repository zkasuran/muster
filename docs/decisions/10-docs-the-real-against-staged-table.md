# Decision: a four-status real-against-staged table sits above the fold

Recorded by `10-DOCS-AND-POLICY.md` section 5.4.

## The decision

The page a judge lands on carries a table naming, per capability, whether it is real on mainnet, real on
testnet, staged or not built. A status may only move down without a product change, so the table is a
commitment rather than a caveat. The first-party share, the measurement methodology plus who is behind the
venue sit in the same disclosure set.

## The alternative rejected

A capabilities page with a caveats footnote, which is what a pitch does.

## Why

A judge reads one page adversarially, so the limitation belongs in the same table as the claim. A footnote
separates them by a scroll and the reader who finds the footnote after the claim trusts the claim less
than if the two had arrived together.

The four statuses also stop the softening that happens under deadline pressure. "Staged" cannot be
upgraded by rewording and a capability that slips becomes a status change anybody can diff rather than a
sentence nobody re-reads.

The same posture governs the incident history plus the rulebook: both ship empty rather than seeded,
because a rulebook that looks used when it is new is the same defect as a seeded shelf.

## What it binds

`10-DOCS-AND-POLICY.md`'s disclosure set, `02-THESIS.md`'s honesty clause on the ship line,
`13-PARTNERS.md`'s report which cannot claim a pair that did not run, `14-GAPS.md`'s empty incident log,
`15-SYSTEM.md` where the submission may not claim what the table does not support.

## What would make us revisit it

Nothing. Statuses move as the build lands, which is the table working rather than the rule changing.
